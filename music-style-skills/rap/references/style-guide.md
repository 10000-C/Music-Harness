# Rap / Hip-Hop Style Guide

## Aesthetic center

In this library, `rap` is shorthand for **writing an instrumental and arrangement that supports rhythmic vocal flow, cadence, hook, and lyrical foreground**.

The durable aesthetic center is **pocket, repetition, sonic identity, negative space, and the relationship between voice and beat**. The beat is not background decoration; it is the compositional environment that makes a flow feel inevitable.

## Choose the lane first

| Lane                     | Core priorities                                                                                 |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Boom-bap                 | drum pocket, sample/loop identity, swing, lyrical space                                         |
| Trap                     | half-time gravity, 808 movement, hat detail, sparse melodic loop                                |
| Drill                    | sliding low end, darker tension, distinctive kick/808 placement, aggressive pocket              |
| Cloud/atmospheric        | haze, spacious loops, softened drums, vocal atmosphere                                          |
| Melodic rap              | vocal melody space, simpler harmonic support, hook-forward arrangement                          |
| Alternative/experimental | unusual samples/timbres, broken form, nonstandard rhythm while preserving a usable vocal pocket |

Do not use trap as the default for every rap request.

## Identity-bearing variables

Prioritize:

1. vocal pocket;
2. kick/snare relationship and subdivision feel;
3. bass/808 contour;
4. loop identity;
5. negative space;
6. 4/8/16-bar arrangement changes;
7. harmonic complexity only when the lane needs it.

## Rhythm and pocket

Boom-bap commonly uses a strong backbeat, syncopated kick variation, and swing or microtiming around an eighth/sixteenth grid. Trap commonly uses half-time snare gravity, selective hat rolls and gaps, kick/808 interaction, and sparse melodic rhythm. Drill commonly uses a strong half-time perception, mobile/sliding bass, lurching kick placement, and active percussion over minimal melodic layers.

Across lanes, **leave rhythmic territory for the voice**. If every subdivision is occupied, the rapper must fight the beat.

## Harmony and pitch language

Useful approaches include one- or two-chord vamps, short loops, dark modal centers, minor-pentatonic or modal fragments, jazz/soul-derived sample harmony in boom-bap and lo-fi lanes, minimal upper harmony above moving 808s in trap/drill, and pitch-shifted or recontextualized textures used as hooks.

Do not add chord changes merely to avoid repetition. Repetition can be the frame that makes flow, lyric changes, and drum edits audible.

## Bass / 808 language

- decide whether the 808 follows the kick, answers it, or sustains through it;
- use slides as phrase events, not on every transition;
- preserve rests so low-end hits retain scale;
- simplify bass when the sample/loop already has a strong low register;
- use octave/register shifts for section energy;
- make the bass phrase memorable enough to function as a hook when the lane depends on it.

## Melody, samples, and loop identity

Possible strategies include short piano/key motifs, chopped soul/jazz textures, bell/pluck/synth cells, guitar loops, atmospheric pads with one foreground motif, vocal fragments used rhythmically, and intentionally sparse one- or two-note figures.

A strong loop is not “unfinished” because it repeats. The question is whether it has enough identity to sustain changing vocal information.

## Arrangement and form

Rap arrangement often works through **subtraction, drops, mutes, and small loop mutations**. Useful 4/8/16-bar moves include removing kick before a hook, muting a sample for one lyric line, introducing alternate hats, changing the 808 ending at section boundaries, adding counterline only in the hook, dropping to drums and vocal, or leaving space for ad-libs instead of adding another instrument.

Traditional 16-bar verses and 8-bar hooks are useful reference units, not mandatory templates.

## Vocal-aware composition

When the vocal exists or is planned, determine whether the flow is dense or spacious, straight or triplet-based, double-time or dragged, melodic or percussive; identify hook type, ad-lib space, and the syllables or phrases where the beat should get out of the way.

A beat that sounds impressive alone can fail once a rapper enters. The arrangement should reserve foreground space.

## Neighboring styles and controlled drift

| Direction    | Increase                                                | Reduce                      |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| R&B          | chord color, sung melody, vocal stacks, smoother pocket | percussive lyrical density  |
| Pop rap      | hook clarity, section contrast, polished arrangement    | long loop stasis            |
| Lo-fi        | swing, dusty texture, soft transients, jazz color       | sub-heavy precision         |
| Electronic   | synthetic timbre, sound-design motion, club energy      | sample-organic feel         |
| Rock/emo rap | guitar loop, live texture, emotional sung hooks         | traditional sample language |

## Anti-patterns

Avoid reducing rap/hip-hop to 808 plus rapid hi-hat regardless of subgenre, over-arranging every four bars, constant kick/808 unison, harmonic movement that destroys the pocket, filling vocal gaps before considering a vocal, identical trap programming across all lanes, foregrounding the instrumental lead over the intended rapper, or treating repetition as a flaw.

## Pre-generation checklist

Before writing, decide:

- Which rap lane is the anchor?
- What is the perceived pulse: straight, swung, half-time, or double-time?
- Where will the vocal sit rhythmically?
- What single loop/motif gives the beat identity?
- How do kick and bass converse?
- Which elements can disappear to create section contrast?
- Where is the intentional empty space?

If the beat has no room for a changing flow, it is not yet functioning as a rap arrangement.
